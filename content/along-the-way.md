My research on [remote work](/research/#remote) and [four-day workweeks](/research/#fourday) studies alternatives to the usual way of working. Doing that work, I came to see the question underneath it: what kind of work do people want, and do employers and society make room for it? And beneath that lies a larger question, one that ties this work together: what kind of life do people hope to live, what do they think is possible, and what place does work have in it? My research on [the meaning of work](/research/#meaning) takes up this question most directly, and so does a new seminar I will teach in spring 2027, [*A Self of One's Own*](/teaching/#a-self-of-ones-own).

The question is also a personal one. For years, I read mostly to learn how to do my work well. Then I began to wonder what my own work was for. Looking for an answer led me to many of the readings below, and later to Vipassana meditation. Vipassana trains attention on bodily sensation rather than on thoughts, feelings, or stories, and it asks me to meet each sensation with equanimity. Meditation has not given me a clear answer, but it has set me on the way toward one. Along the way, I have come to see how much of experience goes unspoken, and how much of it we do not know, or do not know that we do not know. The readings below are some of what I met on that way, listed in the order I read them. Each changed something in me, if only a little.
