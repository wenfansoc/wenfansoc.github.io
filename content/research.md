## 1. The Future of Work

The COVID-19 pandemic profoundly reshaped how, when, and where people work. This line of research examines novel work arrangements that emerged or expanded in its wake — such as remote and hybrid work and four-day workweeks — and how they affect work patterns, family life, health, and well-being across gender, race-ethnicity, and life course stages. This work is supported by three NSF grants, a Russell Sage Foundation grant, and a Canadian Institutes of Health Research grant.

## 4. Life Course

This work investigates the health consequences of macro-level events — political movements and historical shocks — using life course principles of socio-historical location, timing, heterogeneity, linked lives, and human agency, primarily in the Chinese context.
