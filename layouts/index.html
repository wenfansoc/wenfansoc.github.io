I am an Associate Professor in the Department of Sociology at Boston College. What do people want from their work, and what does work do to them in return? My research takes up these questions as work changes—where we do it, how long we spend on it, and what we expect from it—and traces the consequences for health, well-being, and family life in the United States, China, and beyond.

I am currently working on three projects:

  • an NSF-funded study of remote and hybrid work during the COVID-19 pandemic (with Phyllis Moen);
  • a project on the employee, organizational, and societal impacts of four-day workweeks (with Juliet Schor, Orla Kelly, and Phyllis Moen; funded by NSF and the Russell Sage Foundation); and
  • a study of how young people in China understand the meaning of work, drawing on in-depth interviews.

I received my Ph.D. in Sociology and M.S. in Statistics from the University of Minnesota.
